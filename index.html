The idea

One chassis.
Endless cars.

In this concept, every RDM shares one bolt-together platform. Modules would connect with standard mounts and a single data plug, so an upgrade could be a service visit instead of a new car.

  1. PowertrainPetrol, hybrid or electric, all on the same mounts.
  2. Energy floorA fuel tank or a battery pack in the flat floor.
  3. InteriorSeats, dash and screen as one tray — from five seats to cargo.
  4. CornersWheels, brakes and suspension per corner, for city or gravel.
  5. FasciasFront and rear bumpers with lights. Refresh the face, not the car.
  6. RoofSteel, panoramic glass, rails or solar.

Concept lineup

Three bodies.
Named after the years
that shaped Poland.

The three concepts share the same modules, so a powertrain from the RDM 1989 would fit the RDM 966. The shape is chosen once — the rest could change later.

Design

Retro where it counts.
Modern where it matters.

The concept borrows the honest, angular lines of 1970s and 80s Polish cars like the FSO Polonez, and imagines them with pixel-LED lights, wool seats and a screen that knows when to stay quiet.

Upgrade, don't replace

Your car,
version two.

The thinking behind RDM: when one part of a car gets old, replace that part — not the whole car. Old modules would be refurbished and used again.

  1. Year 1

    Start simple

    An RDM 1989 with a small petrol module. Light, simple, first-car energy.

  2. Year 4

    Go hybrid

    A longer commute? Swap in a hybrid module. The old one goes back to be refurbished.

  3. Year 7

    Go electric

    Drop in an electric module and battery floor — same body, same seats, same memories.

  4. Year 10

    Refresh the face

    New fascias, new lights, new wheels. The car looks new because, mostly, it is.

The name

Named after
Radom.

Radom is a city about 100 km south of Warsaw with a long industrial history. This concept imagines it as the home of a car maker that builds its cars once and keeps improving them.

“A car shouldn't be disposable. It should be built to be kept — and improved.”

About this concept

An idea,
not a product.

RDM is a fictional brand and a design exercise in what affordable, modular cars could look like.

  • Not a real company. RDM and Radom Motors don't exist and aren't affiliated with any car maker.
  • Nothing is for sale. The cars haven't been built and there are no prices, orders or test drives.
  • AI-generated images. Every car, person and place in the photos on this page was generated with AI.
  • Real history. The years behind the model names — 966, 1683 and 1989 — are real moments in Polish history.
